# Free & Open Source Ebook Readers for Desktop

> Distraction-free EPUB, MOBI and PDF reading software for Windows, macOS and Linux

Finding a reliable free desktop ebook reader is surprisingly frustrating when so many apps are either bloated library suites, clunky PDF viewers, or commercial storefronts disguised as software. If you read on a laptop or desktop monitor, you need an app that respects your screen real estate, gives you control over margins and line height, and gets out of the way of the prose. Every tool below is completely free to use for reading on your computer.

These tools divide into four practical groups. Modern cross-platform readers like Koodo Reader and Readest combine slick visual shelves with cloud sync across devices. Native desktop readers such as Foliate on Linux and Sumatra PDF on Windows prioritize instant startup, low memory usage, and seamless integration with your operating system. Specialized viewers like YACReader optimize specifically for dual-page comic books and manga archives. Finally, academic and command-line tools like Okular and epy cater to deep annotation workflows and keyboard-centric terminal reading.

Pick the free desktop ebook reader that matches how you read rather than the longest format list. If you read mostly novels, typography and margin controls matter most; if you study research papers or comic books, annotation tools and layout modes should drive your choice.

## How these were chosen

- Free for its core purpose - no expiring trial, no artificial reading limits, and no paywalled reading features.
- Weighted typography control heavily - a reader you spend hours in is chosen on font rendering, line spacing, margins, and hyphenation, not on raw feature checklists.
- Listed by primary job - applications whose main purpose is library cataloguing and batch conversion (like Calibre) belong in our ebook management list and are cross-referenced here rather than duplicated.
- Actively maintained or clearly labelled - older, slower-moving tools like CoolReader are identified transparently in their downsides so you know what to expect.
- Open source transparency - every open-source entry links directly to its source repository so licence terms can be verified in a single click.
- No affiliate links - every link points straight to the official project homepage or source repository.

## Details

### 1. Foliate

**Platforms** Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2019  
[Homepage](https://johnfactotum.github.io/foliate/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/johnfactotum/foliate)

Linux users who want a clean, distraction-free native reader with superb typography

**Good** &mdash; Modern GTK 4 and Libadwaita interface that integrates seamlessly with Linux desktop themes; Fine-grained typography controls for margins, line spacing, justification, and custom CSS fonts; Built-in dictionary lookups, offline translation support, and text-to-speech reading

**Less good** &mdash; Linux only, with no native builds for Windows or macOS; Reading shelf is purely a local file viewer rather than a full catalog manager

Foliate is widely considered the gold standard for reading on the Linux desktop. It was recently rewritten with GTK 4 and a custom rendering engine, making it faster and even cleaner than earlier versions. If you are on Windows or Mac, look at Readest or Koodo Reader instead.

### 2. Thorium Reader

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** BSD-3-Clause &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2017  
[Homepage](https://www.edrlab.org/software/thorium-reader/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/edrlab/thorium-reader)

Readers requiring exceptional accessibility features or standards-compliant EPUB 3 rendering

**Good** &mdash; Maintained by EDRLab, delivering the most faithful EPUB 3 and Readium standard rendering available; World-class accessibility including deep screen-reader support, dyslexia-friendly fonts, and synchronized TTS; Supports fixed-layout EPUBs, audiobooks, and LCP-protected library loans

**Less good** &mdash; Heavier Electron footprint with slower startup times than native viewers like Sumatra PDF; Interface is designed strictly around accessibility and reading rather than library cataloguing

Thorium Reader is the reference reader for the open publishing ecosystem. While its interface can feel utilitarian compared to consumer apps, its adherence to modern web and EPUB standards makes it unmatched for complex layouts, mathematical formulas, and accessible reading.

### 3. Koodo Reader

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux, Web &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** AGPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2020  
[Homepage](https://koodoreader.com/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/koodo-reader/koodo-reader)

Anyone wanting a modern, feature-rich reader with built-in cloud sync for their personal collection

**Good** &mdash; Broad format support covering EPUB, PDF, MOBI, AZW3, CBR, CBZ, FB2, and Markdown; Seamless synchronization of reading progress, highlights, and notes via WebDAV, Dropbox, or OneDrive; Highly customizable typography, background colors, dual-page mode, and continuous scrolling

**Less good** &mdash; Electron-based application that consumes noticeable RAM with large collections; Sync requires configuring your own third-party cloud storage or WebDAV provider

Koodo Reader bridges the gap between a pure reader and a library shelf. It is ideal for readers who want their bookmarks and reading positions to sync across a laptop, desktop, and web browser without relying on proprietary ecosystems like Amazon Kindle.

### 4. Readest

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux, Web &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** AGPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2024  
[Homepage](https://readest.com/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/readest/readest)

Readers wanting Foliate's elegant reading engine packaged into a fast, cross-platform app

**Good** &mdash; Lightweight Tauri v2 desktop shell with significantly lower memory usage than standard Electron apps; Parallel split-screen reading for comparing two books, translations, or study notes simultaneously; Built-in WebDAV synchronization for bookmarks, notes, and reading positions across desktop and mobile

**Less good** &mdash; Rapidly evolving project with frequent interface updates and occasional minor regressions; PDF rendering is experimental compared to its mature, rock-solid EPUB engine

Readest was created to bring Foliate's beloved rendering engine to Windows, macOS, Android, and iOS. By using Tauri and Rust instead of Electron, it stays nimble while offering modern conveniences like text-to-speech, translation, and full cross-device sync.

### 5. Sumatra PDF

**Platforms** Windows &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free, donation-supported &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2006  
[Homepage](https://www.sumatrapdfreader.org/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/sumatrapdfreader/sumatrapdf)

Windows users who want an instant, ultra-lightweight reader for quick book and document lookups

**Good** &mdash; Practically instantaneous startup and microscopic memory consumption on any Windows machine; Opens EPUB, MOBI, FB2, PDF, CBZ, and XPS effortlessly without requiring separate tools; Available as a portable executable requiring zero installation or background processes

**Less good** &mdash; Windows only; Minimalist typography adjustments with no visual font or margin controls outside an advanced text config file

Sumatra PDF is famous as a PDF viewer, but its built-in engine reads EPUB, MOBI, and comic archives just as swiftly. If you prioritize speed and simplicity over fancy animations and cloud features, nothing opens an ebook faster on Windows.

### 6. Arianna

**Platforms** Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2022  
[Homepage](https://apps.kde.org/arianna/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://invent.kde.org/graphics/arianna)

KDE Plasma and mobile Linux users looking for a sleek, responsive native EPUB reader

**Good** &mdash; Native Kirigami and Qt interface that matches KDE Plasma desktops and scales effortlessly to touch screens; Automatic book discovery that scans directories and organizes titles by author, series, and genre; Distraction-free reading view powered by QtWebEngine and epub.js

**Less good** &mdash; Strictly focused on EPUB files, with no support for PDF, MOBI, or comic archives; Windows and macOS builds are experimental and not packaged for general use

Developed as part of the KDE Gear ecosystem, Arianna provides KDE users with a focused, modern reading experience similar to Foliate in GNOME. It pairs library browsing with smooth pagination and respects system color schemes out of the box.

### 7. Okular

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-2.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2005  
[Homepage](https://okular.kde.org/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://invent.kde.org/graphics/okular)

Students and researchers who need deep markup, margin annotations, and study tools across document formats

**Good** &mdash; Comprehensive annotation suite including highlighters, inline notes, stamps, and freehand drawing; Reads almost any document format imaginable, including EPUB, PDF, DjVu, MOBI, and comic archives; Integrated text-to-speech synthesis and high-contrast accessibility color modes

**Less good** &mdash; EPUB layout rendering is less fluid and elegant than dedicated reflowable ebook readers; Feature-dense desktop interface can feel overwhelming for casual novel reading

Okular is KDE's flagship universal document viewer. While it does not provide the cozy typography-first presentation of Foliate or Koodo Reader, its annotation and review capabilities make it essential for reading textbooks, research papers, and technical manuals.

### 8. Anx Reader

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** MIT &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2024  
[Homepage](https://anx.anxcye.com/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/anxcye/anx-reader)

Readers who want detailed reading habits tracking, heatmaps, and customizable reading layouts

**Good** &mdash; In-depth reading statistics including reading speed, session heatmaps, and progress analytics; Fluid Flutter-based UI with extensive typography, theme, and dual-column customization; Full WebDAV synchronization for libraries, highlights, and reading milestones across desktop and mobile

**Less good** &mdash; Relatively new project with occasional interface quirks on desktop platforms; AI features require supplying your own third-party API keys

Anx Reader has rapidly gained traction as a stylish, cross-platform reading companion. If you enjoy tracking your reading habits the way habit apps track daily routines, its built-in analytics and clean layout offer a fresh alternative to older readers.

### 9. YACReader

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free, donation-supported &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2009  
[Homepage](https://www.yacreader.com/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/YACReader/yacreader)

Comic, graphic novel, and manga readers who need double-page spreads and smooth navigation

**Good** &mdash; Tailored specifically for visual reading with double-page spread, right-to-left manga mode, and page rotation; Advanced image enhancement controls for brightness, contrast, and gamma correction on scanned pages; Includes 'Go To Flow' quick visual browsing to jump across chapters and story arcs

**Less good** &mdash; Designed for comic archives (CBZ, CBR, PDF); EPUB support is limited to experimental image-based comic EPUBs, not reflowable text EPUBs or MOBI files; Companion library server is useful but adds extra complexity if you only want a standalone viewer

Reading comic archives in a general-purpose ebook reader is often frustrating because of page sizing and orientation issues. YACReader solves this with native support for dual-page spreads, manga direction, and image filtering across all major desktop systems.

### 10. Bookworm

**Platforms** Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2017  
[Homepage](https://babluboy.github.io/bookworm/) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/babluboy/bookworm)

Minimalist Linux readers who prefer a focused, distraction-free reading window without extra bells

**Good** &mdash; Uniform, clean reading interface across EPUB, PDF, MOBI, FB2, and comic formats; Distraction-free full-screen mode with two-page spread, font zoom, and search; Lightweight memory footprint on elementary OS and Ubuntu-based desktop distributions

**Less good** &mdash; Tagged releases have been dormant for several years, though the codebase remains functional; Linux only, with installation primarily geared toward Debian/Ubuntu PPAs, Flatpak, and source builds

Bookworm was created for elementary OS to deliver a calm, book-first reading view free of clutter. While development velocity has slowed compared to newer apps like Foliate, it remains a reliable, lightweight option for reading multiple formats on Linux.

### 11. CoolReader

**Platforms** Windows, Linux &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-2.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2000  
[Homepage](https://github.com/buggins/coolreader) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/buggins/coolreader)

Older or low-resource computers that need granular font smoothing and hyphenation control

**Good** &mdash; Extensive typographic fine-tuning including multi-language hyphenation dictionaries and custom CSS rules; Extremely low memory footprint that runs effortlessly on legacy PCs and low-power laptops; Supports obscure and retro ebook formats like FB2, RTF, CHM, and plain text alongside EPUB

**Less good** &mdash; Official binary releases have been infrequent, and the user interface feels noticeably dated; No modern cloud sync or integrated mobile ecosystem

CoolReader (built on the crengine core) is one of the oldest surviving open-source reading engines in existence, and actually served as the foundation for early KOReader releases. If you need hyphenation rules and font styling on an old netbook or low-spec laptop, CoolReader still delivers.

### 12. epy

**Platforms** Windows, macOS, Linux, CLI &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Licence** GPL-3.0 &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Cost** Free &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; **Established** 2019  
[Homepage](https://github.com/wustho/epy) &nbsp;&middot;&nbsp; [Source](https://github.com/wustho/epy)

Terminal and keyboard enthusiasts who want to read EPUBs without leaving the command line

**Good** &mdash; Complete keyboard-driven navigation with customizable vim-like keybindings; Zero graphical overhead, running inside any standard terminal on Linux, macOS, or Windows; Supports external terminal dictionary lookups (sdcv/dict) and reading progress indicators

**Less good** &mdash; Command-line only with no mouse interaction, image rendering, or visual formatting options; Effectively finished rather than actively developed: the last tagged release was December 2022 and the last commit March 2024. A known crash on Python 3.13 (the removed imghdr module) is still unfixed and needs a manual dependency workaround.

For developers, sysadmins, and writers who spend their entire working day inside a terminal, opening a heavyweight graphical app just to read a book is disruptive. epy runs directly in your shell, preserves reading progress across sessions, and lets you read without ever taking your fingers off the keyboard. Development has gone quiet, so users on Python 3.13+ currently need to install the standard-imghdr backport package manually to keep it running.

## FAQ

**What is the best free desktop ebook reader for Windows?**

Sumatra PDF is the best choice if you prioritize sheer speed, instant startup, and low memory usage for EPUB, MOBI, and PDF files. For readers who want advanced typography, custom themes, and cross-device sync, Koodo Reader and Readest offer modern, feature-complete reading environments.

**Why use a dedicated desktop ebook reader instead of a web browser or Calibre?**

While modern browsers can open PDFs and some EPUBs through extensions, they lack essential reading features like custom hyphenation, margin padding, page spreads, and offline annotation export. Calibre is an outstanding library manager, but its built-in viewer is heavy and detached from the operating system's native look and feel. Dedicated readers provide distraction-free windows optimized specifically for long reading sessions.

**Can these desktop readers open Kindle (AZW/MOBI) or comic files?**

Yes, DRM-free MOBI and AZW3 files are supported by several tools here, including Foliate, Koodo Reader, Readest, and Sumatra PDF. For comic books and manga packaged in CBZ or CBR archives, YACReader and OpenComic provide specialized dual-page layouts, manga right-to-left orientation, and image optimization controls.

**How do I sync my reading position between my desktop and mobile phone?**

Readers like Koodo Reader, Readest, and Anx Reader support cloud synchronization through open protocols like WebDAV or cloud storage providers such as Dropbox and OneDrive. By pointing both your desktop app and mobile companion to the same storage folder, your bookmarks, highlights, and reading progress update automatically without locking you into a proprietary ecosystem.

**Do any of these free desktop ebook readers strip DRM from purchased books?**

No. Every application in this guide respects digital rights management and is designed strictly to read DRM-free books or openly licensed formats like EPUB 3. Bypassing DRM is restricted by copyright laws in many jurisdictions, so none of these tools include decryption features.
